Responsible Disclosure Policy

We welcome careful, good-faith reports that help us protect clients, visitors, and our operations.

1. Report a potential vulnerability

Email stonewardsecurity@gmail.com with the subject “Security vulnerability report.” Include:

  • The affected URL, service, or asset.
  • A clear description of the issue and its potential impact.
  • Minimal, reproducible steps or a proof of concept using your own test data.
  • The date and time observed, relevant request IDs, and your preferred contact method.

Do not send credentials, private keys, live personal data, full raw logs, or weaponized exploit code in the first email. Describe what you found and we will coordinate a safer transfer method if needed.

2. Scope

This policy covers the public Stoneward Security website and systems clearly owned and operated by Stoneward. Third-party platforms—including GitHub, Web3Forms, Google, and their infrastructure—are not in scope unless the issue is caused by Stoneward’s configuration and can be demonstrated without testing the provider itself.

Client systems, data, networks, accounts, collectors, and engagement environments are out of scope unless you have separate, explicit written authorization naming the asset and permitted testing.

3. Good-faith research guidelines

  • Use the minimum testing needed to confirm a potential issue.
  • Use accounts and data you own or have explicit permission to use.
  • Avoid accessing, changing, retaining, downloading, or disclosing other people’s data.
  • Stop testing and report promptly if you encounter sensitive information or evidence of active compromise.
  • Give us a reasonable opportunity to investigate and address a validated issue before public disclosure.
  • Follow applicable law and this policy.

4. Prohibited actions

This policy does not authorize:

  • Denial-of-service, traffic flooding, resource exhaustion, or destructive testing.
  • Social engineering, phishing, spam, physical intrusion, or harassment.
  • Password spraying, credential stuffing, brute force, or use of leaked credentials.
  • Malware deployment, persistence, pivoting, or access beyond what is minimally necessary.
  • Data exfiltration, privacy violations, public disclosure of sensitive data, or extortion.
  • Testing third-party or client systems without their explicit written authorization.

5. What to expect

We will make reasonable efforts to acknowledge a complete report within three business days, validate the issue, keep you informed when practical, and prioritize remediation based on risk. Complex issues or third-party dependencies may take longer. We may ask for clarification or stop testing that creates risk.

Please do not treat the absence of a response as permission to continue intrusive testing or disclose sensitive information.

6. Good-faith assurance

When research follows this policy, remains within scope, avoids harm, and is reported promptly, Stoneward will consider it authorized security research under this policy and will not initiate legal action solely because of that research. If a third party initiates action, we may clarify that the activity was conducted under this policy when we reasonably conclude that it was compliant.

This assurance does not bind third parties or law enforcement, excuse violations of law, authorize access to client or provider systems, or waive rights concerning harmful, reckless, deceptive, or out-of-scope conduct. If you are unsure whether a test is permitted, ask before proceeding.

7. Recognition and rewards

This is not a bug-bounty program, and no payment or reward is promised. Any recognition is voluntary and requires the reporter’s permission. Do not make public claims that Stoneward has validated an issue until we confirm that in writing.
