Data Handling Policy

Our default is simple: collect less, limit access, retain only what is needed, and keep raw logs on your network when requested.

1. Handling principles

  • Minimum necessary: request only data reasonably needed for the approved purpose.
  • Written authorization: define scope, systems, accounts, and permitted actions before technical work begins.
  • Need-to-know access: limit access to the people and tools required for the engagement.
  • Separation: keep client materials logically separated and avoid mixing data between engagements.
  • Time-limited retention: delete or return technical data when it is no longer needed or as the agreement requires.

2. Public website and inquiry intake

Do not submit passwords, one-time codes, private keys, API secrets, Social Security numbers, financial or health information, sensitive personal information, evidence files, or raw security logs through the public form or unsolicited email.

The public form is for basic business contact details, service selection, and a high-level environment description. Form submissions are processed by Web3Forms and delivered to Stoneward’s Google email account. See our Privacy Policy for details.

3. Client engagement data

Before accepting technical data, Stoneward and the client should agree on the purpose, permitted sources, transfer method, retention expectations, and people authorized to receive findings. The client is responsible for confirming it has authority to provide the systems and data in scope.

We ask clients to minimize or redact personal information and secrets where they are not needed. If unexpected sensitive data appears, we will stop or isolate handling as appropriate and coordinate a safer path with the client.

4. Analysis options

Stoneward analysis workspace

When the written scope permits temporary transfer, authorized logs or evidence are placed in a restricted analysis workspace. Raw materials are used only for the engagement, then deleted at the end of the analysis run unless the client and Stoneward agree in writing to another period. Findings and the report may be retained separately as described below.

Client-site analysis

When selected and technically feasible, a code-signed collector runs in the client’s environment so raw logs do not leave the client’s network. Only findings or an agreed summary are transferred to Stoneward. The collector package can be verified with a published SHA-256 digest. IP masking and secret redaction may be configured when included in scope.

Actual tooling, architecture, supported data sources, and validation procedures are confirmed in the statement of work. This public policy does not authorize access to any system.

5. Safeguards

Depending on scope and data sensitivity, safeguards may include encrypted transport, multifactor authentication, least-privilege accounts, restricted workspaces, device encryption, checksum verification, secret redaction, access logging, and secure deletion procedures. No safeguard eliminates all risk.

Stoneward does not use client technical data to train public artificial-intelligence models or for advertising. Any automated analysis or third-party tool that may receive client data must be disclosed or authorized in the engagement scope.

Deletion from active systems may not immediately remove protected backups, provider security logs, legal holds, or records we must keep. Those copies remain restricted until their normal expiration.

7. Service providers

Public website providers currently include GitHub Pages, Web3Forms and Google. Engagement-specific tools or subprocessors, if any, are identified in the written scope when their use is relevant to client data. We evaluate the purpose and data flow before introducing a new provider.

8. Security incidents

If Stoneward identifies suspected unauthorized access to client data under our control, we will take reasonable steps to contain and investigate it, preserve relevant evidence, coordinate with affected providers and the client, and provide notices as required by applicable law and the client agreement. Incident details may be limited while an investigation or legal restriction is active.

9. Client requests and questions

Authorized client contacts may request return, correction, access review, or deletion of engagement data, subject to the agreement, identity verification, applicable law, and technical limitations.
